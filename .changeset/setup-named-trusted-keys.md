`describeSetup`, pure and on the setup client, now names the trusted keys of the deployment's two identity methods. Each entry of `SetupDescription.parties.methods` (`DescribedParties`) gains the optional last member `namedKeys`, of the new type `NamedTrustedKeys`, decoded from the `trustedParties()` declaration the client already reads at the description's pinned block, with no new read. On the descriptor's `methodZkpassport` it is `{ kind: 'method-zkpassport', version, rootVerifier, subVerifier, verifierHelper, rootRegistry, rootAdmin, rootGuardian, subVerifierAdmin }`, the eight words in the method's order: `version` is word 0 as `major.minor.patch`, decoded by `zkPassportVersionOf`, and every other member is its word's low 20 bytes, checksummed. `rootAdmin`, `rootGuardian` and `subVerifierAdmin` are the method's last-reviewed snapshot of the root verifier's admin and guardian and of the sub-verifier's admin, not a read of those upstream contracts, which may have moved since; `rootGuardian` is the zero address where the root had none. On the descriptor's `methodAadhaar` it is `{ kind: 'method-aadhaar', issuerKeyHash }`, its one word in lower case. The member is absent where the read was not answered, on every other method, and where the words do not pass that method's own on-chain acceptance rule (zkPassport: exactly eight 32-byte words, the version nonzero and zero past its first six bytes, every other word zero in its upper 12 bytes and nonzero but for the root guardian; Aadhaar: exactly one nonzero word); deciding it never throws and raises no finding, and `parties` keeps the raw words. New constants: `DESCRIPTION_ZKPASSPORT_KEY_NAMES` (the eight member names, index = word), `DESCRIPTION_ZKPASSPORT_OPTIONAL_KEY` (6, the root guardian's word) and `DESCRIPTION_ISSUER_KEY_NAMES` (`issuerKeyHash`).

Compatibility: `namedKeys` is optional, so a consumer that reads the description or builds one by hand needs no change. An entry of any other method, or whose words do not decode, is byte-identical to before, as are `validateSetup`, its findings, the setup client's reads and every closed set. `describeSetup` now refuses with a `TypeError` a hand-built context whose `descriptor.methodZkpassport` or `descriptor.methodAadhaar` is not an address.
