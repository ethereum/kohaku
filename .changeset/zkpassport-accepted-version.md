The zkPassport method refuses a proof of another version than the module accepts, where the integrator passes the module's trusted keys. `enrollInput` and `signingInput` take the optional param `trustedKeys`, the module's `Parties.trustedKeys` exactly as the integrator read them from `trustedParties`: at enrollment through the setup reads, before signing through the approval page's own provider. The session keeps them lower-cased as the new optional member `ZkPassportSession.trustedKeys`, and both calls throw a `TypeError` with the new `METHOD_ZKPASSPORT_TRUSTED_KEYS_MESSAGE`, before any stack call, unless they are an array of exactly `METHOD_ZKPASSPORT_TRUSTED_KEY_COUNT` (8) 32-byte hex words whose word `METHOD_ZKPASSPORT_VERSION_KEY` (the first) `zkPassportVersionOf` reads. Where they are given, `configFrom` and `replyFrom` compare the version the stack's `getSolidityVerifierParameters` returns with that word by value, and answer the new frozen record `METHOD_ZKPASSPORT_MATERIAL_UNTRUSTED` on a mismatch, after the dev-mode echo check and before the identifier and scope checks; `replyFrom` still answers `request-unsupported` first, and a version the stack returns malformed is `material-rejected`. An input record whose `trustedKeys` are malformed is `material-rejected`, as one without a session is. New: the reply-failure cause `material-untrusted` in `REPLY_FAILURE_CAUSES`, after `material-rejected`, for material that proves, but under an issuer key or version the method's module does not trust now. The SDK makes no new read. `verify`, the codec, the vectors, `describe` and every call without `trustedKeys` are unchanged, and a proof refused for its version does not invalidate the credential: a proof from an app on the accepted version still approves.

Compatibility: the closed set `REPLY_FAILURE_CAUSES`, and with it the `ReplyFailureCause` union, grew by one member, so a consumer that keeps an exhaustive map or switch over every cause must add `material-untrusted` before it compiles again; the methods orchestrator passes it on from `configFrom` and `replyFrom` like the other causes. A page names the accepted version with `zkPassportVersionOf` on the word and the app's with the outer proof's `version`. An integrator that enrolls while the module's version is being rotated omits `trustedKeys` at enrollment.
